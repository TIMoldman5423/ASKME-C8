C8 Ask – read me

A small working example of putting C8 data in a database and asking it questions by voice or text. Written as an aid for directors and senior management only, while work continues on the larger corporate product. Working draft of this document, last updated 28 October 2026.

Status at 28 October 2026

Financial questions it can answer now: profit and loss against budget, balance sheet, bank debt, cash and the finance team's commentary for each monthly Finance Board Report loaded (with an estimated cash flow, derived because the reports have no cash flow statement); and the FY27 budget, which is final and Board-approved (network sales, revenue, costs, profit, Marketing Concepts, monthly cash flow, headcount and assumption notes). Board papers are held for searching, and weekly sales are held by store for 65 weeks (27 July 2025 to 18 October 2026).

What is missing or limited:

  • Finance reports are not every month. The monthly reports loaded so far have gaps (the list is on the data status answer). Missing months need the PDFs from SharePoint.
  • Orders and average transaction value are held for the latest week only. Power BI's weekly store table ignores its Orders/ATV selector, so the history cannot be scraped; it needs direct dataset access.
  • Sales by channel (in-store, Uber, DoorDash and so on) by store by week is not held. It could be scraped slowly, but we are waiting for direct access.
  • Unit sales are not held (Power BI's quantity measure is unreliable for combos and promotions).
  • Item sales cover the top items per brand (about the top 50), monthly by store for 13 months, in dollars only. They do not add up to the weekly sales report.
  • Network Tracker was last loaded from the 17 June 2026 version; a current copy is needed.
  • Virtual brands (Supreme Leader Chicken, Double Diamond Dumplings, wings and others) are not held.

The gaps above are parked until direct Power BI dataset access is sorted out (being explored through C8's existing Copilot work). Last updated 28 October 2026.

What it is

A web app that lets directors ask plain-English questions about C8 ("how did Hawthorn trade last week?", "who reports to Tom?", "what is budgeted for royalties?") and get a short spoken-style answer. The numbers sit in a Postgres database. Claude (Anthropic's AI) only decides which lookup to run and then words the result.

Question (voice or text)→Server→Claude picks a lookup→Database returns exact rows→Claude words a short answer

The key design choice: Claude never writes SQL and never answers from memory. It can only call a fixed list of read-only lookups (store profile, sales history, ranking, group totals, events, item sales, org chart, budget, data status). Every figure in an answer must come from a lookup result in that conversation. If the data does not hold the answer, it says so.

What is in the database now

  • Stores: about 190 stores with brand, state, address, area manager, trading company, opening date (with a reliability label), plus the other names people use for each store.
  • Weekly sales: 65 weeks by store (27 July 2025 to 18 October 2026) from the Power BI sales report, with orders and average transaction value for the latest week only. A restated figure keeps its old value in a history table.
  • Monthly finance reports: profit and loss against budget, balance sheet, bank debt, cash and the finance team's commentary for each month loaded, plus an estimated cash flow and free cash flow for any month (derived, because the reports have no cash flow statement).
  • Development pipeline (Network Tracker): every new-store, transfer, closure and renewal row with its status, dates, delays and step-by-step checklist (lease signed, design, fitout, training and so on), the activity log, and the C8-held lease list. The latest dated entry in the tracker is June 2026, and it is refreshed by re-loading the tracker about every two weeks.
  • Network events: openings, transfers, closures, agreement and lease dates (2025–26 tracker).
  • Item sales: dollars by menu item (top items per brand), by store and by month for 13 months, across all six brands, plus brand totals; Noodle Box also has some weekly reports. Captured from the Power BI Item Sales report. Not the same basis as weekly sales, so the two are never added or compared.
  • Organisation chart (names, positions, reporting lines only – no salaries or contact details).
  • FY27 budget: network sales, revenue, costs, profit, Marketing Concepts, monthly cash flow, headcount, and about 20 short assumption notes.
  • Questions asked: every question, what was looked up and whether it was answered, reviewable by admins.

How data gets in

  • Admin page (/admin): upload a CSV (or the budget JSON). The file type is recognised from its header. A preview shows matches, unmatched names and whether the stores add up to the report's own total before anything is saved.
  • Most uploads replace only their own slice (a brand and period, the whole chart, one budget version), so re-uploading is safe.
  • Financial figures: because Claude does the heavy lifting of reading and preparing the reports, we hope to refresh the financial figures about every two weeks, with each new report loaded through the same admin page.
  • Every upload and edit is logged on the History tab.
  • Weekly item sales were gathered by automating the Power BI report page, one store at a time, with totals checked against the report. That works but is slow and fragile; it is not how a production feed should work.

Security

  • Because we have built a database which sits outside the main domain of the company, we have added the extra overhead of two-way security checks, which will significantly lessen the risk of data escaping.
  • Sign-in is by individual email address plus a random 6-digit code (valid 5 minutes, single use, five tries, limited sends). Admins add and switch off people on the Users tab. Sessions last 12 hours by default, and each person can be given a shorter or longer time (1 hour to 30 days) on the Users tab.
  • Sign-in record. Every attempt to sign in is kept (the address typed, what happened, the internet address it came from, the time), including strangers who are not on the list. They get no code and are told nothing, but they show up under Admin → Sign-ins and in the deployment log, which gives evidence if it is ever needed. Kept for about a year.
  • Access classes. Only people on the users list can sign in at all. Inside it, each person is in a class (Board, Finance only, Operations) that lists the areas they may ask about: sales and stores, the organisation chart, company financial statements, finance commentary, executive and director pay, the budget and forecast, and the board papers. A new person starts in Operations. Administrators and the Board class see everything. The limits are enforced on the server: tools outside a person's class are not offered to Claude and are refused if called, the matching windows are hidden, and the “what data is held” answer describes only what they may use. You tick the areas for each class on the Users tab.
  • The database password, email password and AI key live only in Railway settings, never in the code or in chat.
  • Claude has read-only lookups only. Admin pages need an admin sign-in. Questions and answers are logged.

What we have learned

  1. Data quality first. Most effort went into matching names (stores are called different things in different reports), checking totals, and labelling weak data (for example opening dates that are really agreement dates).
  2. A fixed catalogue of lookups beats letting the AI write SQL. It is safer, cheaper, easier to test, and the answers are repeatable. Add a lookup when the question log shows a real gap.
  3. Keep different bases apart. Weekly sales, item sales and budget are different measures. Say which one an answer uses and never mix them.
  4. Show before you save. Previews with reconciliation to the source report caught most problems early.
  5. Speed. The database answers in milliseconds; most of the wait is the AI's few seconds of thinking, so even complex questions feel quick.
  6. Log what goes wrong. The Questions tab lists unanswered questions. That list is the best guide to what data to add next.
  7. Get a proper feed. A bigger system should read from a scheduled export or data warehouse (for example the Azure data lake mentioned in the budget) rather than uploaded files or screen automation.

Known limits

  • Sales history is 13 weeks; item sales cover Noodle Box only and a handful of weeks.
  • Voice depends on the browser (Safari or Chrome over https).
  • One database, one small server: fine for a handful of directors, not sized for the whole company.

Who sees what

Each person is in an access class. Board (directors, CFO, CEO) has every area. Finance only has sales, the org chart and the financial statements, but not the finance team commentary, executive and director pay lines, the budget or the board papers. Operations has sales and the org chart. New people start in Operations. The board papers, written commentary and pay lines are confidential to directors, the CFO and the CEO, so keep those people in the Board class and everyone else out of it.

Board papers

Admin → Board papers: choose the board pack, Finance Board Report, minutes and agenda PDFs (any number at once) and press Load. Each paper is kept page by page and Claude writes a short summary with key points and decisions and their page numbers. Ask things like “what did the board decide about Ingle Farm?” or “key points in the latest board pack”; the answer names the paper, the page and the file. Summaries are machine-written, so the original paper is the reference, and figures still come from the finance tables. Loading a paper again replaces it; “Summarise again” re-makes a summary; “Link to original” adds a shared Dropbox link. Roughly 15 to 30 seconds per paper, so leave the page open for a big batch. Load summaries file (same page) takes a ready-made summaries.json: papers already held are updated at once, and for the rest the summary waits and is used when you load the PDF, so no call to Claude is needed.
