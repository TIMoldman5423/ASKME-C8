C8 Ask – read me

A small working example of putting C8 data in a database and asking it questions by voice or text. Written as an aid for directors and senior management only, while work continues on the larger corporate product. Working draft of this document, last updated 12 November 2026.

How dates and periods work

  • Weeks run Monday to Sunday and are named by the Sunday they end. “Last week” and “this week” mean the latest complete week held (shown above), so on a Monday or Tuesday “last week” is still the week that ended the Sunday before, until the new weekly file is loaded.
  • Days: “yesterday”, “last Friday” and “the latest day” are worked out from the latest day held in the daily data, not from today’s date. Ask names the day and date it used.
  • Week so far (week to date) comes from the daily sales, day by day, from Monday to the latest day held. It is on the daily basis, which differs from the weekly report by about 3.5%, so it is never added to or compared with weekly figures.
  • Like-for-like (LFL) compares a store with the same week a year earlier (364 days back, so the weekday matches), counting only stores that traded in both weeks. The daily version compares with the same weekday last week and a year earlier.
  • Month and year to date: finance figures are for a calendar month from the monthly Finance Board Report, and “this year” or “year to date” means the financial year, which runs July to June. A year named FY27 is July 2026 to June 2027.
  • Item sales are held for a period (a month, or a block of weeks) and every answer says which one. They are not on the same basis as weekly sales.
  • Every answer should say the week, day, month or period it is using. If it does not, ask it.

What is missing or limited:

  • Finance reports are not every month. The months held are in the table above. Missing months need the PDFs from SharePoint.
  • Orders and average transaction value in the weekly figures are held for the latest week only. The daily figures hold orders for every day.
  • Sales by channel (in-store, Uber, DoorDash and so on), time of day, loyalty and labour are not held. For those, ask ConceptMate.
  • Unit sales are held only where an item sales result says so (Power BI’s quantity measure is unreliable for combos and promotions).
  • Item sales for a store usually cover only the store’s top items, and some periods hold only the top 30 items nationally; the answer says when a result is partial.
  • Network Tracker answers are only as current as its latest entry (shown above); entries after that date are plans, not facts.
  • Weekly and daily data are loaded by an admin from ConceptMate exports, normally each Wednesday for the week just ended. Until then the latest week stays as shown above.

Where Ask fits next to ConceptMate

The two tools do different jobs and work best side by side.

ConceptMate (CM) is incredibly powerful, but slower and costlier. It works across all of C8's data and can answer almost any question, including ones nobody planned for: deep analysis, comparisons, drill-downs and combinations of data that Ask does not hold. It builds each answer from scratch, so each question takes longer and uses far more computing. It suits senior managers and complex questions, not routine look-ups by many staff.

C8 Ask is the quick and basic tool. It answers a fixed set of common questions from data loaded in advance: weekly and daily sales, rankings, like-for-like, item sales, store profiles, and the director-only material (finance reports, budget, board papers, pipeline, franchise agreements, organisation chart, manuals). Answers take seconds and cost very little, because the figures come from ready-made database look-ups and the AI only words the answer. It can go no deeper than the data loaded, and small differences from the source reports are accepted for speed. For an exact figure, ask CM. Access is limited to approved directors.

Which to use. Ask for the basics: how a store or brand did, who is top this week, what sells at a store, what the board decided, what the budget says. CM for anything deeper or unusual, any figure that must be exact, and any data Ask does not hold. When Ask says it does not hold something, that is the cue to ask CM.

Keeping costs down. Routine questions go to Ask and CM is kept for the questions only it can answer. Ask's sales data comes mostly from CM exports, so CM stays the source of truth. Last updated 10 November 2026.

Reading and marking board papers

Press Board papers, choose a paper and read it in Ask. Select words to highlight (yellow, green, amber, orange or red), circle, underline or strike them through, or add a comment or a question. Tap ¶ Sentence first to mark the whole sentence round your selection. Or say, for example, “highlight bank debt”, “circle bank debt in red”, “colour the sentence containing lease provision green”, “comment on page 4: ask Patrick about the lease”, “list my questions”. Send my questions emails your marked questions (with page and quoted line, never the paper) to one chosen director. Your marks are private to you, stored encrypted, and the paper itself is never changed or emailed. Papers loaded before this feature need loading once more to be readable here.

My library: your own material

Press My library to load your own text: a Word (.docx), .txt, .md or .csv file, or paste some text. It is split into entries (up to 300 per file, 8,000 characters each) so Ask can find the right part quickly. Then ask about it in the usual way, for example “what is in my library about the Hawks fixtures?”. You can load anything you like, not only work material. It is private to you and the system administrator: no other user can read it. It is not encrypted (it has to be searchable), so only load material you are happy for an administrator to be able to see. When you ask about it, the matching text is sent to the AI to word the answer, and those questions are left out of the question log. Old .doc files must be saved as .docx first. CSV files need a title column and a text column. Loading a file with the same name replaces it; Delete removes it.

Your private notes and reminders

Type or say note, to do or remind me to followed by what to remember (for example “remind me to ring Patrick tomorrow at 3pm”), or press the Ask / Note button so everything you type is saved. Say list my notes to hear them, delete note 3, mark 2 done. The Notes button opens your list. Reminders are emailed to you at the due time. Only you can see or hear your notes, they are stored encrypted, and they never go to Claude.

What it is

A web app that lets directors ask plain-English questions about C8 ("how did Hawthorn trade last week?", "who reports to Tom?", "what is budgeted for royalties?") and get a short spoken-style answer. The numbers sit in a Postgres database. Claude (Anthropic's AI) only decides which lookup to run and then words the result.

Question (voice or text)→Server→Claude picks a lookup→Database returns exact rows→Claude words a short answer

The key design choice: Claude never writes SQL and never answers from memory. It can only call a fixed list of read-only lookups (store profile, sales history, ranking, group totals, events, item sales, org chart, budget, data status). Every figure in an answer must come from a lookup result in that conversation. If the data does not hold the answer, it says so.

What is in the database now

  • Stores: about 190 stores with brand, state, address, area manager, trading company, opening date (with a reliability label), plus the other names people use for each store.
  • Daily sales: sales and orders by store by trading day, from the Power BI Daily Sales dataset via ConceptMate exports (a rolling run of recent days, see the table above for the days held). The daily dataset differs from the weekly report by about 3.5%, so the two are never added or compared.
  • Weekly sales: weeks by store (from July 2025, extended each week; see the table above) from the Power BI sales report, with orders and average transaction value for the latest week only. A restated figure keeps its old value in a history table.
  • Monthly finance reports: profit and loss against budget, balance sheet, bank debt, cash and the finance team's commentary for each month loaded, plus an estimated cash flow and free cash flow for any month (derived, because the reports have no cash flow statement).
  • Development pipeline (Network Tracker): every new-store, transfer, closure and renewal row with its status, dates, delays and step-by-step checklist (lease signed, design, fitout, training and so on), the activity log, and the C8-held lease list. The latest dated entry is shown in the table above; it is refreshed by re-loading the tracker about every two weeks.
  • Network events: openings, transfers, closures, agreement and lease dates (from the tracker).
  • Item sales: dollars (and units where held) by menu item, top items per brand, by store and by period (see the table above for the periods each brand holds), across the 14 brands including the virtual brands (Double Dragon Dumplings, Bokyo, Supreme Leader and others), which are kept separate from their host brand. Loaded from ConceptMate exports. Not the same basis as weekly sales, so the two are never added or compared.
  • Organisation chart (names, positions, reporting lines only – no salaries or contact details).
  • FY27 budget: network sales, revenue, costs, profit, Marketing Concepts, monthly cash flow, headcount, and about 20 short assumption notes.
  • Questions asked: every question, what was looked up and whether it was answered, reviewable by admins.

How data gets in

  • Admin page (/admin): upload a CSV (or the budget JSON). The file type is recognised from its header. A preview shows matches, unmatched names and whether the stores add up to the report's own total before anything is saved.
  • Most uploads replace only their own slice (a brand and period, the whole chart, one budget version), so re-uploading is safe.
  • Financial figures: because Claude does the heavy lifting of reading and preparing the reports, we hope to refresh the financial figures about every two weeks, with each new report loaded through the same admin page.
  • Every upload and edit is logged on the History tab.
  • Weekly item sales were gathered by automating the Power BI report page, one store at a time, with totals checked against the report. That works but is slow and fragile; it is not how a production feed should work.

Security

  • Because we have built a database which sits outside the main domain of the company, we have added the extra overhead of two-way security checks, which will significantly lessen the risk of data escaping.
  • Sign-in is by individual email address plus a random 6-digit code (valid 5 minutes, single use, five tries, limited sends). Admins add and switch off people on the Users tab. Sessions last 12 hours by default, and each person can be given a shorter or longer time (1 hour to 30 days) on the Users tab.
  • Sign-in record. Every attempt to sign in is kept (the address typed, what happened, the internet address it came from, the time), including strangers who are not on the list. They get no code and are told nothing, but they show up under Admin → Sign-ins and in the deployment log, which gives evidence if it is ever needed. Kept for about a year.
  • Access classes. Only people on the users list can sign in at all. Inside it, each person is in a class (Board, Finance only, Operations) that lists the areas they may ask about: sales and stores, the organisation chart, company financial statements, finance commentary, executive and director pay, the budget and forecast, and the board papers. A new person starts in Operations. Administrators and the Board class see everything. The limits are enforced on the server: tools outside a person's class are not offered to Claude and are refused if called, the matching windows are hidden, and the “what data is held” answer describes only what they may use. You tick the areas for each class on the Users tab.
  • The database password, email password and AI key live only in Railway settings, never in the code or in chat.
  • Claude has read-only lookups only. Admin pages need an admin sign-in. Questions and answers are logged.

What we have learned

  1. Data quality first. Most effort went into matching names (stores are called different things in different reports), checking totals, and labelling weak data (for example opening dates that are really agreement dates).
  2. A fixed catalogue of lookups beats letting the AI write SQL. It is safer, cheaper, easier to test, and the answers are repeatable. Add a lookup when the question log shows a real gap.
  3. Keep different bases apart. Weekly sales, item sales and budget are different measures. Say which one an answer uses and never mix them.
  4. Show before you save. Previews with reconciliation to the source report caught most problems early.
  5. Speed. The database answers in milliseconds; most of the wait is the AI's few seconds of thinking, so even complex questions feel quick.
  6. Log what goes wrong. The Questions tab lists unanswered questions. That list is the best guide to what data to add next.
  7. Get a proper feed. A bigger system should read from a scheduled export or data warehouse (for example the Azure data lake mentioned in the budget) rather than uploaded files or screen automation.

Known limits

  • Sales history starts in July 2025 and item sales start in May 2026, so year-on-year comparisons for some periods are not possible; Ask says so.
  • Voice depends on the browser (Safari, Chrome or Edge over https; Firefox has none). If the microphone does not work, Ask now says why. On an iPhone or iPad check Settings > Safari > Microphone, Settings > Privacy & Security > Speech Recognition (allow Safari) and Settings > General > Keyboard > Enable Dictation. Speak straight after pressing the button, because the browser stops listening after a short pause.
  • Charts are either bars or lines, not both on one graph, and share one scale. A sales-and-percentage combination (bars with a line on a second axis) is a possible later addition.
  • Download as a spreadsheet. Every chart, and every list answer from the sales tools (top stores, a store's weekly history, totals, item sales, pipeline), has a Download CSV button that opens in Excel or Numbers. Ask for "top 20 stores as a spreadsheet". Finance, board, legal, pay, notes and library answers have no download.
  • One database, one small server: fine for a handful of directors. Extending it company-wide would need more user classes, owned accounts and an automated data feed.

Who sees what

Each person is in an access class. Board (directors, CFO, CEO) has every area. Finance only has sales, the org chart and the financial statements, but not the finance team commentary, executive and director pay lines, the budget or the board papers. Operations has sales and the org chart. Other areas, such as the operating manuals, are switched on per class on the Users tab. New people start in Operations. The board papers, written commentary and pay lines are confidential to directors, the CFO and the CEO, so keep those people in the Board class and everyone else out of it.

Board papers

Admin → Board papers: choose the board pack, Finance Board Report, minutes and agenda PDFs (any number at once) and press Load. Each paper is kept page by page and Claude writes a short summary with key points and decisions and their page numbers. Ask things like “what did the board decide about Ingle Farm?” or “key points in the latest board pack”; the answer names the paper, the page and the file. Summaries are machine-written, so the original paper is the reference, and figures still come from the finance tables. Loading a paper again replaces it; “Summarise again” re-makes a summary; “Link to original” adds a shared Dropbox link. Roughly 15 to 30 seconds per paper, so leave the page open for a big batch. Load summaries file (same page) takes a ready-made summaries.json: papers already held are updated at once, and for the rest the summary waits and is used when you load the PDF, so no call to Claude is needed.
